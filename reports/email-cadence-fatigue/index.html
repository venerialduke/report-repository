Send more email only to people who read it

In a 12-week, 450,000-subscriber cadence test, going from 2 to 4 emails a week raised revenue by 21%. Going to 6 added almost nothing, burned through the list and cost us revenue once the test ended. A cadence set by engagement beats any single frequency.

By Lena Kowalski Published Feb 25, 2026 Data through Jan 25, 2026

The short version

  1. Returns flatten after 4 emails a week. Moving from 2 to 4 sends a week lifted 12-week revenue per recipient by +$0.85 (+21%). Moving from 4 to 6 added only +$0.17, and that difference isn't statistically significant.
  2. Six a week borrows from the future. Unsubscribes tripled (1.9% → 6.1%), and the share of people clicking fell week after week. In the 9 weeks after the test, when every arm was back on the normal cadence, the 6/wk arm earned 15% less than the 2/wk arm.
  3. Heavy readers want more email and lapsed readers want less. Recipients who clicked in the last 30 days earned more at every step up to 6/wk. Recipients with no click in 90+ days earned the least at 6/wk.
  4. Recommendation: roll out engagement-based cadence (6 / 4 / 2 a week by tier). We estimate +$4.6M a year in revenue with no more unsubscribes than today. Owner: CRM team, live by 1 Apr.

Revenue per recipient counts all revenue from a recipient in every channel, not just email-attributed orders. Because recipients were randomised, the differences between arms are causal and include email's halo onto direct and app traffic.1

01Each extra email is worth less, and after 4 a week close to nothing

Emails 3 and 4 each week earned about 3.5¢ per send. Emails 5 and 6 earned under 1¢, too little to cover the unsubscribes they caused.

The 2→4 step added 24 sends per recipient over 12 weeks and +$0.85 in revenue. The 4→6 step added the same 24 sends and only +$0.17 (95% CI −$0.08 to +$0.42). The extra emails mostly moved purchases between sends rather than creating new ones. Customers who would have bought after Tuesday's email bought after Monday's instead.

02At 6 a week, engagement starts high and keeps falling

We call this the fatigue curve: the share of recipients who click at least one email in a given week.

The 6/wk arm started with the most weekly clickers (14.6%), since more emails give more chances to click. It lost about a third of them by week 12 and finished barely above the 2/wk arm (9.7% vs. 9.2%), despite sending three times as many emails. The 4/wk arm declined more slowly, and the 2/wk arm barely moved. Because we measure clicks rather than opens, Apple Mail Privacy Protection doesn't distort the curve.

03After the test ended, the heaviest-mailed arm earned the least

The costs of over-mailing show up after the campaign. Once we put all three arms back on the standard 3/wk cadence, the 6/wk arm earned $0.55 less per recipient than the 2/wk arm over the next nine weeks.

Two things drove this. The 6/wk arm had lost 6.1% of its list to unsubscribes, and those people can't be emailed again, including during Black Friday. Those who stayed also clicked less often. Over the full 21 weeks, 4/wk comes out ahead of 6/wk by $0.26 per recipient (95% CI −$0.03 to +$0.55). The 6/wk arm's $0.17 edge over 4/wk during the test was more than wiped out afterwards.

“I love your stuff but I was getting an email every single day. I don't need a new throw pillow every day.”Unsubscribe survey, 6/wk arm (one of 1,412 free-text responses; 44% mentioned frequency)

04The right frequency depends on how engaged the recipient is

Across the whole list the curve is flat, but engagement tiers pull in different directions. Heavy readers keep earning us more up to 6/wk. Lapsed readers earn less the more we send them.

We split recipients by recency of their last email click at randomisation. The high tier (clicked in the last 30 days) makes up 22% of the list and earned +$1.50 more at 6/wk than at 4/wk. Their unsubscribe rate stayed low at 2.1%. The low tier (no click in 90+ days) makes up 47% of the list. It earned about the same at 2 and 4/wk, dropped to $0.55 at 6/wk, and 8.6% of the tier unsubscribed.

What engagement-based cadence gets us

Average volume stays close to today (3.5 sends a week vs. 3.0), unsubscribes stay flat (2.75%) and revenue per recipient rises about 15%. Across 1.6M promotional subscribers that is roughly +$4.6M a year (90% CI $2.9–6.3M). The low tier gets fewer emails than today, which should also help inbox placement.

Two caveats

First, today's 3/wk figure is interpolated between arms, not tested directly. Second, the engagement-based design itself hasn't been tested. Subscribers will move between tiers as their behaviour changes, which a fixed-arm test can't capture. We recommend launching it with a 10% holdout on flat 3/wk (see #2).

05Recommendations

  1. Launch engagement-based cadence: 6 / 4 / 2 emails a week by tier, with tiers recalculated weekly from click recency. Owner: CRM team (Rosa Lindqvist) · By: Apr 1, 2026
  2. Keep a 10% holdout on the current flat 3/wk cadence for 12 weeks to confirm the modelled +15% before it goes into the plan. Owner: Marketing Science (Lena Kowalski) & Experimentation (Priya Natarajan) · Readout by: Jul 15, 2026
  3. Sunset subscribers with no click in 180+ days to a 1/wk "best of" digest, and suppress them from peak-season blasts. Owner: CRM team · By: May 1, 2026
  4. Report 21-week revenue per recipient and list health next to campaign revenue in the CRM dashboard, so the long-run cost of volume is visible every week. Owner: Analytics Engineering (Jonah Feld) · By: Apr 15, 2026

Methodology

Population
US promotional email subscribers who were active (at least one open or click in the last 365 days) on 25 Aug 2025: 1.60M. We drew 450,017 at random and randomised them 1:1:1 to arms, stratified by engagement tier and Pine+ status. Transactional and Pine+ lifecycle emails were unchanged.
Period
Test 1 Sep–23 Nov 2025 (12 weeks, ending before Black Friday). Follow-up 24 Nov 2025–25 Jan 2026 (9 weeks), with all arms on the standard 3/wk cadence.
Treatment
Arms received the same campaign calendar. The 2/wk arm got the two highest-priority sends each week, and the 4/wk and 6/wk arms added the next priorities in order. Content was held constant.
Analysis
Intention-to-treat on all randomised recipients, including those who later unsubscribed. We compared revenue per recipient with Welch's t-test, winsorised at the 99.9th percentile, and computed CIs by bootstrap (2,000 resamples). The annual projection scales the 12-week gain by 52/12 and assumes the tier mix stays stable.
Limitations
Autumn test period, so the effect may differ in peak or January. Tier-level estimates are subgroup analyses: they were pre-registered, but each tier has less power than the full test. We can't observe revenue lost when customers mark email as spam without unsubscribing. Unsubscribes are a lower bound on list damage.

1 Email-attributed revenue (last-click within 3 days) was 31% of the measured revenue difference between the 2/wk and 4/wk arms. Measuring on attributed revenue alone would have understated the value of the 3rd and 4th emails.

Test pre-registered in the experimentation log as EXP-2025-081. Thanks to Rosa Lindqvist and the CRM team for running three parallel campaign calendars for 12 weeks.
