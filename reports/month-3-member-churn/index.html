Monthly Pine+ members churn twice as fast in month 3, and a third order is the best early warning

The drop comes once the free-shipping honeymoon ends. Members who place a third order within 60 days mostly stay, so the time to step in is month 2.

By Daniel Reyes Published Sep 15, 2026 Data through Aug 31, 2026

The short version

  1. Month 3 is where monthly members leave. Of monthly-plan members still active when month 3 starts, 18.6% churn before month 4. The average for months 1, 2 and 4 is 8.4%. Annual-plan members show no such cliff.
  2. It is a habit problem, not a price problem. Month-3 churn is 28.8% for members who placed only their joining order in the first 60 days and 6.1% for those who placed three or more. After matching on channel, basket and region, a gap of 9.8 pp remains.
  3. Promo-driven joiners are the most fragile. Members who joined in the Black Friday promotion churn at 26.8% in month 3. Organic joiners churn at 11.7%.
  4. Recommendation: test a month-2 “third order” nudge and a month-2 annual-conversion offer. We estimate that together they could save about 3,000 members and about $0.6M in contribution a year. Owners: Retention squad and Membership product.

01Monthly members leave in a sharp spike at month 3, not gradually

Churn is usually pictured as a steady leak. For monthly Pine+ members it looks more like a cliff. The hazard more than doubles in the third month of tenure, then drops back to its earlier level.

We followed every member who joined Pine+ between September 2025 and March 2026: 96,400 people, of whom 52,100 (54%) chose the $6.99 monthly plan and 44,300 the $59 annual plan. For each month of tenure we measured the hazard, meaning the share of members active at the start of that month who had churned (voluntarily or involuntarily) before the next one began.

The timing points to a cause. By month 3 a monthly member has been billed three times (up to $20.97 in all). Members who join to save on shipping for one large basket have usually used up that value by the second renewal. Our cancel-flow survey says the same thing: 38% of month-3 cancellers chose “I don’t order often enough to make it worth it” (section 04).

02Annual members don’t have a month-3 problem; monthly members lose almost half by month 6

The survival curves split early and never meet again. Six months after joining, 55.4% of monthly members are still active, compared with 96.8% of annual members. The annual “churn” here is almost entirely refund requests within the 30-day guarantee. Their real test comes at the 12-month renewal, which we cover in the Pine+ LTV forecast.

This is not a like-for-like comparison of plans

People choose their plan, so annual members were more committed from day one. The gap shows who picks each plan. It is not evidence that switching someone to annual makes them stay. This is why recommendation 2 is an experiment, not a rollout.

03Promo-driven joiners are the most fragile; organic joiners hold up best

Acquisition channel is a strong predictor of the month-3 drop. Members who signed up during the Black Friday–Cyber Week promotion (when the monthly plan’s first month is discounted to $1) churn at 26.8% in month 3. Members from paid social churn at 24.1%. Organic / direct joiners churn at 11.7%, less than half the Black Friday promo rate. The checkout upsell is our largest channel at 41% of monthly joiners, and it sits in the middle at 16.4%.

04A third order within 60 days is the clearest early signal of who stays

We tested about 40 candidate early signals, including app installs, wishlist adds, email engagement and category breadth. The most useful was simple: did the member place a third order (counting the joining order) within 60 days of signing up?

Only 29% of monthly joiners do. Their month-3 churn is 6.1%. For members who place just a second order it is 17.9%, and for members who never order again after joining it is 28.8%. As a classifier, the flag alone gets an AUC of 0.71 for month-3 churn. Our full gradient-boosted model reaches 0.76, so the single flag gives up little and is far easier to use.

This does not prove that a third order causes retention. Engaged people both order more and stay longer. To separate the two, we matched members with and without a third order on acquisition channel, first-basket value and category, region, device and join month (propensity-score matching, 11,200 pairs). The raw gap is 17.6 pp. After matching it shrinks to 9.8 pp (95% CI 7.7–11.9 pp). That is still large, and it is the effect size we would design an intervention around.

So what

Most month-3 churners are not unhappy members. They are members who never built a habit. That points to intervening in month 2, while there is still time to earn a third order, rather than offering a discount at the cancel screen once the decision is made.

05Recommendations

We sized each option on about 6,200 monthly members who reach month 3 each month. Cutting the month-3 hazard by 4 pp would keep about 250 more members a month, roughly 3,000 a year. At an average incremental contribution of about $200 per retained member over the following 12 months (FP&A’s Pine+ unit-economics model), that is worth about $0.6M a year.

  1. Test a month-2 “third order” nudge. Trigger a personalised email and app message on day 35 for monthly members with fewer than three orders, with category recommendations based on the first basket and a reminder of the free shipping they have used. Randomise 50/50 and use month-3 churn as the primary metric. At 6,200 members a month, 8 weeks detects a 2.5 pp effect at 80% power. Owner: Retention squad with CRM · Launch by: Oct 20, 2026 · Read-out: Jan 2027
  2. Test an annual-conversion offer in month 2. Offer the annual plan at $49 for the first year (normally $59) to monthly members on day 40–50. Measure 12-month retained revenue, not just switches, so we don’t pay to convert members who would have stayed anyway. Owner: Membership product with FP&A · Design sign-off by: Oct 31, 2026
  3. Stop leading with the $1 monthly offer during sale events. Show the annual plan first on sale-day checkout and a clearer comparison of the two. Black Friday promo joiners churn at 26.8% in month 3, so part of what counts as sale-period member growth doesn’t last. Owner: Growth marketing · Before: Black Friday 2026 member campaign
  4. Add “3rd order in 60 days” to the weekly membership dashboard as a leading KPI next to new joiners, so the effect of changes shows up about 30 days before churn does. Owner: Insights (Jonah Feld) · By: Oct 9, 2026

Methodology

Population
All new Pine+ members who joined between Sep 1, 2025 and Mar 31, 2026 (96,400). Excludes employee memberships, gifted memberships and members re-joining within 90 days of a previous cancellation (4.1% of joiners). Each joiner is observed for 6 full months of tenure.
Definitions
Churn follows the canonical definitions in the metric glossary. A member churns on the date access ends, not the date they click cancel. Involuntary churn is a payment failure that is not recovered within the 14-day retry window.
Method
Kaplan–Meier survival curves by plan and channel; monthly hazards computed on members at risk at the start of each tenure month. Leading indicators were screened with univariate AUC and then a gradient-boosted classifier (5-fold CV). The effect of a third order was estimated by 1:1 nearest-neighbour propensity matching (caliper 0.05 SD), with bootstrap CIs from 1,000 resamples.
Limitations
Matching adjusts only for what we observe. Unmeasured intent (for example, an upcoming house move) probably still inflates the 9.8 pp estimate, which is why we recommend testing before scaling. Channel is last-touch at sign-up. The cancel-flow survey is optional (41% response) and single-choice.

1 Involuntary churn in month 3 (3.3 pp) is higher than in month 2 (2.1 pp), partly because cards used for a one-off large purchase are more likely to be declined later. The payments team is testing card-updater coverage separately.
