Paid social pays back, but retargeting doesn't

A 28-market geo holdout shows paid social returns $2.15 per dollar, well under the 5.6× the platforms report. Nearly all of the gap is in retargeting, so we recommend moving $3.4M of it into prospecting and new tests.

By Lena Kowalski Published Jun 3, 2026 Data through May 24, 2026

The short version

  1. Paid social is incremental, just less than we thought. Turning it off in 14 markets for six weeks cut revenue there by 4.1% (95% CI 2.7–5.5%). That puts incremental ROAS (iROAS) at 2.15× (CI 1.42–2.88×), against 5.6× in platform reporting. The platforms over-credit by about 2.6×.
  2. Prospecting clears our hurdle rate and retargeting doesn't. Prospecting returned 2.81× incrementally. Retargeting returned 0.97×, meaning most of those buyers would have bought anyway. Only about 1 in 8 retargeting conversions the platforms claim is actually incremental.
  3. Recommendation: cut retargeting by 60% ($5.7M → $2.3M a year). Move $2.4M into prospecting and hold $1.0M for a paid search holdout in Q3. We expect about +$2.0M of incremental revenue a year at the same total spend. Owner: Performance Marketing.

01Switching paid social off cost the dark markets about 4% of revenue

For eight weeks before the test, the two groups of markets moved together within a point of each other. Once paid social went dark, the holdout markets fell behind, and the gap stayed open for the full six weeks.

We paused all Meta, Pinterest and TikTok delivery in 14 designated market areas (DMAs) from 6 April to 17 May 2026. The other 14 DMAs, matched on pre-period revenue trend, Pine+ penetration and delivery footprint, kept running business as usual. The outcome was total revenue across web, app and phone orders shipped to each DMA. That captures the halo onto direct and organic traffic that platform attribution never sees.

The gap grew from about 3 points in week 1 to 5 points by week 4. That fits an adstock effect: people exposed to ads in March kept converting for a couple of weeks after we went dark. A synthetic-control model puts the average revenue shortfall across the test at 4.1% (95% CI 2.7–5.5%), or $3.95M of revenue on $1.84M of withheld spend.

02The platforms claim 2.6× more revenue than paid social actually drives

Across the same markets and weeks, the platforms' own attribution (7-day click, 1-day view) credited paid social with 5.6× ROAS. The holdout says 2.15×.

Some over-crediting is normal. View-through windows count anyone who scrolled past an ad and later bought, and each platform counts the same order independently. How much the platforms over-credit, though, depends heavily on the tactic, and the budget decisions depend on that split.

We split the 14 dark markets into two phases. For the first two weeks both tactics were paused. For the last four weeks retargeting alone was switched back on in 7 of the 14 markets. Comparing those 7 with the 7 that stayed fully dark isolates the retargeting effect. The prospecting effect is what remains.1

03Only about one in eight retargeting conversions is incremental

Retargeting audiences are people who viewed a product or abandoned a cart in the last 14 days. Most of them were coming back anyway.

Put another way: of every 100 orders Meta attributes to a retargeting ad, about 12 would not have happened without it. For prospecting the figure is 67. That matches what the Customer squad sees in session data. The median retargeted buyer had already made 3.4 visits before seeing the ad, and 58% returned through direct or branded search, not the ad click.

Why the dashboards got this wrong

Retargeting targets people who are already close to buying, so it will always look efficient in last-touch or platform attribution. The ad gets credit for an order that was coming anyway. That makes it a measurement problem more than a creative or bidding problem, and better optimisation inside the platform won't fix it.

04Moving $3.4M out of retargeting should add about $2M in revenue at the same total spend

Retargeting shouldn't go to zero. The top of the retargeting audience (cart abandoners in the last 3 days) likely still earns its keep. But current spend is well past the point of diminishing returns.

The 2026 paid social budget is $16.0M, of which $5.7M (36%) is retargeting. We propose cutting retargeting to $2.3M, restricted to 3-day cart abandoners. $2.4M of the savings would go to prospecting and $1.0M to a paid search geo holdout in Q3. Paid search is the next-largest channel we have never tested for incrementality.

Extra prospecting spend won't return the 2.81× we measured on average. Using the response curve from our marketing mix model, we assume 2.2× on the marginal dollar. That gives +$5.3M from prospecting against −$3.3M lost from retargeting, for a net +$2.0M a year (80% range +$0.6M to +$3.4M). The range doesn't count any value from the paid search test.

What this doesn't tell us

The test ran in spring. Retargeting may do relatively better at peak, when more people are comparison-shopping between sites. We recommend a smaller retargeting-only holdout in November before committing the Q4 split.

Methodology

Design
Matched-market geo holdout. We ranked 210 DMAs on 52 weeks of revenue history and paired them on trend correlation, revenue size, Pine+ penetration and Pine Fleet coverage. We kept the 14 best-matched pairs and randomised which member of each pair went dark. We excluded the 6 Pine Fleet home metros except Phoenix and Columbus, which were matched to each other. The 28 DMAs cover 19.9% of US revenue.
Period
Pre-period 9 Feb–5 Apr 2026 (8 weeks). Test 6 Apr–17 May 2026 (6 weeks). We watched a one-week cool-down to 24 May for residual effects and found none detectable after week 1 of it.
Estimation
Augmented synthetic control on weekly net revenue by DMA, with confidence intervals from placebo-in-time and placebo-in-space permutations (1,000 draws). A difference-in-differences cross-check gave 4.3% (CI 2.6–6.0%).
Hurdle rate
1.8× revenue iROAS, set by FP&A. This is contribution-margin breakeven on first-order revenue plus expected 12-month repeat revenue from new customers.
Limitations
(1) Spring seasonality only; peak may differ. (2) National brand and TV spend continued in all markets, so we measure paid social's effect on top of that baseline, not in isolation. (3) The retargeting estimate rests on 7 vs. 7 markets over 4 weeks and has a wide CI (0.21–1.73×). Even its upper bound is below the hurdle. (4) Spillover from people who live in one DMA and shop in another would bias the estimate slightly downward.

1 Prospecting incremental revenue = total holdout effect − retargeting effect, scaled to spend. The two estimates share a control group, so their CIs are not independent. We bootstrapped the blended CI directly and did not sum the tactic CIs.
