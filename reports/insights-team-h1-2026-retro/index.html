Insights H1 2026 retro: our best work came from planned projects, but ad-hoc requests took almost half our time

What the team shipped, which decisions it changed, what we learned, and what we will do differently in H2, starting with a single home for our work.

By Maya Okafor, Head of Insights Published Jul 8, 2026 Covers Jan 1 – Jun 30, 2026

The short version

  1. We shipped 12 published pieces and answered 312 ad-hoc requests with a team of nine.
  2. A few planned projects drove most of the impact. The checkout test and the carousel holdout alone account for about $30M of annualised revenue that leadership has acted on.
  3. Ad-hoc work grew from 41% to 46% of team time between Q1 and Q2, and 38% of those requests asked something we had already answered.
  4. H2: launch the Insights Hub, run intake through triage and office hours, and keep ad-hoc work to about a third of our time.

01What we shipped

In the first half of the year the team published nine reports and three methods articles, ran or supported 23 experiments, and answered 312 ad-hoc requests. The published pieces were:

  • Product & Digital: the single-page checkout A/B test and the “Complete the room” carousel holdout (Priya Natarajan).
  • Customer & Growth: the 2026 customer segmentation refresh (Daniel Reyes, Priya Natarajan).
  • Operations: the warehouse pick-rate benchmark across our four FCs (Tom Becker).
  • Marketing: email cadence and fatigue, and the paid social geo holdout (Lena Kowalski).
  • Finance & Pricing: the free-shipping threshold elasticity read after the March move to $75 (Samuel Ortiz).
  • People & Workplace: hybrid office utilisation and the 2026 engagement survey (Hannah Liu).
  • Methods & Standards: the experimentation playbook, the “active member” metric definition (Jonah Feld), and a guide to reading confidence intervals (Priya Natarajan).

02The impact came from a handful of decisions

We only count impact when a named decision-maker changed course because of our work, and we use the conservative planning figure, not the point estimate.

  • Checkout: the single page shipped to 100% of web on June 23. FP&A has booked $20M of annualised revenue, and the app gets a v2 test instead of a risky full rollout.
  • Recommendations: the holdout confirmed about $9.6M a year of truly incremental revenue, changed how the squad sets its OKRs, and moved the carousel test onto the cart page.
  • Free shipping: the elasticity work gave Finance its first measured read on the $75 threshold ahead of the H2 pricing review.
  • Media: the paid social geo holdout gave Marketing an incrementality benchmark to use in H2 budget planning.
  • Standards: 19 of the 23 experiments were pre-registered under the new playbook, up from roughly a third last year.

Almost all of this came from work we planned in January. None of it came from the ad-hoc queue.

03What we learned: we were too available

Saying yes to every question felt helpful, but by Q2 it was taking up almost half of the team's time.

We log every request in the intake board, and time tracking is rough but consistent. Both show ad-hoc work growing through the half, from 141 requests in Q1 to 171 in Q2. The time went to one-off pulls, “can you just check this number” questions and deck support.

The last column is the uncomfortable one. More than a third of requests asked something we had already answered in a report, a dashboard or a metric definition. The requester simply couldn't find it. Our work was spread across slide decks, Drive folders, Slack threads and personal notebooks. A good analysis that no one can find does not get used.

Other lessons from the half:

  • Pre-registration works. Tests with a written decision rule reached a decision a median of 6 days after readout. Tests without one took 19 days.
  • Mixed methods beat either method alone. Pairing Aisha Rahman's research with log analysis (checkout, and now app search) made the findings far easier to act on.
  • Conservative impact numbers built trust. FP&A now asks us for the planning figure rather than discounting our numbers themselves.
“When the answer is easy to find, people stop asking us for it, and we can spend that time on the questions nobody has answered yet.”Maya Okafor, team offsite, June 2026

04H2 priorities

  1. Launch the Insights Hub. One searchable home for every report, with metadata, code links and comments, built on the shared report template. All H1 work is migrated first. Owner: Jonah Feld & Maya Okafor · By: Aug 31, 2026
  2. Triage intake and hold twice-weekly office hours. One rotating analyst triages every request. Anything answerable from the Hub gets a link within a day. Target: ad-hoc work at or below 33% of hours by Q4. Owner: Maya Okafor · Starts: Jul 20, 2026
  3. Self-serve metric layer. Certify the top 25 metrics (starting with “active member”) in the semantic layer so common questions can be answered without us. Owner: Jonah Feld · By: Oct 31, 2026
  4. Planned H2 portfolio. Month-3 member churn, Pine+ LTV forecast for FY27, the 2026 peak capacity plan, last-mile SLA review, returns root causes and app search relevance. Owners: as assigned in the H2 roadmap · Through: Dec 2026
  5. Hire a second Marketing Science analyst to meet demand for incrementality work before peak. Owner: Maya Okafor with Lena Kowalski · Offer by: Sep 30, 2026
