The $75 free-shipping threshold is paying for itself, at a cost to new customers

Five weeks after we raised the non-member threshold from $49 to $75, basket-building and Pine+ sign-ups more than cover the lost orders. We estimate +$8.7M a year in contribution. The loss falls on first-time buyers, and that needs fixing.

By Samuel Ortiz Published Apr 8, 2026 Data through Apr 5, 2026

The short version

  1. Shoppers build baskets to reach $75. The share of non-member orders between $75 and $99 jumped from 12% to 20%. Non-member AOV rose +12.6% net of trend (95% CI 10.9–14.3%).
  2. Some shoppers walk away instead. Non-member conversion fell −6.8% relative to the marketplace control (CI −9.1 to −4.5%). New customers took most of the hit (−10.4%).
  3. More people join Pine+. Checkout sign-ups rose 23% after adjusting for seasonality, about 720 extra members a week.
  4. Net effect: +$8.7M a year in contribution (80% range +$3.9M to +$13.5M). Revenue per non-member session is up 4.9%.
  5. Recommendation: keep $75, add a "you're $X away" add-on module in cart, and test a first-order $49 threshold for new customers. Owners: Pricing Committee, Checkout squad, Growth.

01Shoppers moved their baskets up to the new threshold

Before 2 March, the $49–74 band was the second most common basket size for non-member orders. Since the change it has shrunk by 9 points, and the $75–99 band has grown by 8.

Most of the shift comes from baskets padded to qualify for free shipping. Of non-member orders landing between $75 and $85, 34% had an item under $15 added in the final cart session. Before the change, 11% of orders in that range did. The most common add-ons were candles, dish towels and storage baskets, which carry above-average margin. A smaller group of shoppers who used to pad their baskets to $49 now just pay for shipping, which is why the $25–48 band grew 2 points.

02Conversion dropped about 7% against the control, and new customers took most of it

Some shoppers who faced a shipping fee on a $60 basket left without buying. Raw non-member conversion fell from 2.31% to 2.12%. After removing the seasonal dip seen in the control, the drop attributable to the threshold change is 6.8%.

Marketplace orders are our control. Marketplace shoppers see the same catalogue and seasonal demand, but shipping on those orders follows the marketplace's rules, so our threshold change doesn't affect them. The two series tracked within 1–2 index points for the eight pre-period weeks, which supports the parallel-trends assumption.1

Why the new-customer loss matters more than the numbers show

A lost first order also loses the customer's future orders, not just that one sale. We've included a lapsed-customer value line of −$2.4M in the bridge below, based on 12-month repeat rates for first-order customers. It is our least certain line and could be twice as large. We'll re-estimate it in Q3 once we can observe 90-day repeat behaviour.

03The higher threshold is working as a Pine+ ad

Pine+ gives free shipping on every order. At $75, the $59 membership looks like a better deal to a frequent mid-basket shopper, and more of them are joining at checkout.

Raw sign-ups rose 26%, from about 3,100 to 3,900 a week. Adjusting for the 2% seasonal lift seen in the same weeks last year, the threshold effect is +23% (95% CI +15 to +31%), or about 720 additional members a week. Annualised, that is roughly 37,000 members. We value each at $41 of first-year contribution after the shipping subsidy, which gives about $1.5M.2

04Bigger baskets and shipping fees outweigh the lost orders

Lost orders cost us about $14M a year in gross margin. Larger baskets, shipping fees now collected on $49–74 orders and new Pine+ members bring in about twice that.

What drives the range

The 80% range (+$3.9M to +$13.5M) comes mostly from two assumptions: how much margin the add-on items carry, and how many lost new customers never come back. Even at the pessimistic end of both, the change is net positive.

05Recommendations

  1. Keep the $75 threshold. Re-read in Q3 with 6 months of data and observed 90-day repeat rates for the new-customer cohorts. Owner: Pricing Committee (Samuel Ortiz to present) · Re-read by: Jul 31, 2026
  2. Add a "you're $X away from free shipping" module to cart, recommending high-margin add-ons under $20. This should raise the share of shoppers who build up to $75 rather than leave. Owner: Checkout squad with Experimentation (Priya Natarajan) · A/B test live by: May 15, 2026
  3. Test free shipping at $49 on a customer's first order to recover some of the −10.4% new-customer conversion loss. Owner: Growth team · Test design by: May 1, 2026
  4. Promote Pine+ in cart when a basket is between $49 and $74 ("Free shipping on this order and every order for $59/yr"). Owner: Membership team · By: May 15, 2026
  5. Update the FY26 forecast for higher non-member AOV, lower order count and higher Pine+ acquisition. Owner: FP&A · By: Apr 30, 2026

Methodology

Population
Treated group: non-member sessions and orders on web and app (US). Control: orders placed through marketplace channels, which are not subject to our free-shipping threshold. Pine+ members were excluded from the treated group, since their shipping is always free. A secondary check using members as a control gave similar AOV (+12.1%) and conversion (−7.3%) estimates.
Period
Pre-period 5 Jan–1 Mar 2026 (8 weeks). Post-period 2 Mar–5 Apr 2026 (5 weeks). Presidents' Day week was kept in, since it affected both groups similarly.
Method
Difference-in-differences on weekly metrics with week and channel fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered by week, and 95% CIs come from a wild-cluster bootstrap. For bunching, we compared the observed basket-value distribution against a smoothed counterfactual fitted outside the $65–95 window.
Limitations
(1) Five post-weeks is short. Novelty effects may fade, and long-run customer loss isn't observed yet. (2) Marketplace shoppers differ from our web and app shoppers, so the control handles seasonality and demand shocks but not differences in customer mix. (3) The St. Patrick's Day textile promotion (16–22 Mar) ran on web and app only. Excluding that week moves the AOV estimate by less than 0.3 pts. (4) Contribution figures use average margins and could differ at the SKU level.

1 An event-study version with weekly leads showed no significant pre-trend (joint test p = 0.64).
