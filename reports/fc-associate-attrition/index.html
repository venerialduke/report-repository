Nearly half of new FC associates who leave in their first 90 days are gone within two weeks, and unstable schedules are the biggest cause

38% of new associates leave within 90 days, costing about $2.3M a year. The risk is highest in the first two weeks, and it is highest for people whose shifts change at short notice, who have long commutes, or who train in large groups. We propose two pilots to run before peak.

By Hannah Liu & Tom Becker Published Jul 22, 2026 Hires Jan 2025 – Mar 2026, outcomes through Jun 29, 2026

The short version

  1. 38% of the 2,140 FC associates we hired left within 90 days. The rate ranges from 34% in Reno to 45% in Dallas. Weekend/flex shifts lose 49% of hires and day shifts 31%.
  2. There is a cliff in the first two weeks. 17.5% of all hires are gone by day 14, which is 46% of everyone who leaves within 90 days. Weekly exits then fall to about 2% of the cohort.
  3. Three things we control predict early exits. A shift change at short notice in the first 14 days (hazard ratio 1.71), a trainer ratio worse than 1:8 in onboarding week (1.44) and a commute over 30 minutes (1.33). Having a pay rate above the local market median made no significant difference.
  4. Early attrition costs about $2.3M a year ($3,500 per leaver). Dallas accounts for 38% of it.
  5. Recommendation: before peak hiring begins, run a buddy programme (randomised by shift team in Dallas and Columbus) and a 21-day schedule guarantee in Dallas. Together they cost about $200k and target a reduction of 8 points or more.

01The first two weeks decide whether a new associate stays

We usually quote 90-day attrition as a single number. Tracking it week by week shows that most of the damage happens very early.

11% of new associates are gone by the end of week one, and 17.5% by the end of week two. After that the curve flattens: from week 3 to week 13, about 2% of the starting cohort leaves each week. Put another way, the weekly risk of leaving falls from 11% in week one to under 3% of those still employed from week 3 onwards. That tells us where to concentrate retention effort: onboarding and the first two schedules, not 60-day check-ins.

02Dallas and weekend shifts lose the most people, and the FC profiles show why

The FC with the most schedule changes, the largest training groups and the longest commutes also has the highest attrition.

Dallas hired the most associates (690) because it has grown fastest since the 2025 mezzanine expansion. Its trainer ratio of 1:11 reflects the high hiring volume. Its schedule-change rate was already the highest in the network, and it rose further after February 2026, when Dallas moved to a rotating weekend pattern: 58% of Dallas hires since then have had a short-notice change. The same change shows up as an 8-point fall in Dallas engagement in this year's survey.

03Schedule stability, trainer ratio and commute explain most of the risk. Pay above market does not

Once we control for FC, shift, month of hire and experience, the risk factors we can change stand out.

We fitted a Cox proportional-hazards model to time-to-exit over the first 90 days. Controls were FC, shift, hire month (to absorb peak seasonality), prior warehouse experience and whether the hire was a referral.1 The strongest factor is a shift change with less than 7 days' notice in the first 14 days. It affected 31% of hires and raises the hazard of leaving by 71%. 51% of those hires left within 90 days, compared with 32% of the rest. A trainer ratio worse than 1:8 in onboarding week (40% of hires) and a commute over 30 minutes (40% of hires) each raise the hazard by a third or more.

Exit interviews tell the same story in associates' own words. Of the 312 leavers who completed one (38% of leavers), the most common primary reason was that hours or schedule were not what they expected at hire. Physical demands came second, and commute third. Only 14% said they left for another job.

What this means for peak

The Peak 2026 capacity plan assumes about 1,050 seasonal and permanent FC hires between August and November. At today's rates, around 400 of them will be gone within 90 days, most before they are fully productive. Every point of 90-day attrition we remove saves about 10 hires in the peak window.

04Each early leaver costs about $3,500, or $2.3M a year across the network

About a third of the cost is lost productivity from new starters who leave before they reach standard pick rates. The rest is recruiting, onboarding and overtime.

05Two pilots, designed so we can measure the results

We recommend testing before rolling out. Each pilot targets one of the risk factors above and has a clean comparison group.

At a 40% baseline, 500 hires per arm gives about 80% power to detect an 8-point drop in 90-day attrition, allowing for clustering by shift team (ICC ≈ 0.02). The buddy programme is randomised within each site, so the Dallas schedule guarantee applies equally to both arms and the two effects can be measured separately. If both pilots succeed and a combined 8-point reduction holds across the network, we avoid about 137 early leavers a year. That is about $480k a year in direct cost against about $200k of one-off pilot spend, and it would also reduce the recruiting load at peak.

What the pilots can't fix

Physical demands (21% of exit reasons) and commute (17%) are only partly within our control. The Dallas commute finding mostly reflects hires from the south side of the metro, where DART bus service doesn't reach the site. We are costing a shuttle option separately, and it is not part of these pilots.

Methodology

Population
All 2,140 hourly FC associates hired into permanent or seasonal-to-permanent roles from Jan 1, 2025 to Mar 31, 2026 at Reno, Dallas, Columbus and Allentown. Agency temps, rehires within 12 months and internal transfers are excluded.
Outcome
Separation of any kind (resignation, job abandonment or termination) within 90 days of start date. Involuntary terminations are 9% of the 818 exits. Excluding them does not change the pattern of findings.
Risk factors
Schedule changes come from wfm.fct_shift_assignments (a change is any involuntary shift or day change posted less than 7 days ahead). Trainer ratio is new starters per certified trainer in onboarding week. Commute is drive time from home ZIP centroid to site at shift start, estimated with a routing API.
Model
Cox proportional hazards with FC and shift as strata and robust standard errors clustered by hire week. We checked the proportional-hazards assumption with Schoenfeld residuals; the schedule-change effect is strongest in weeks 1–3.
Privacy
Analysis is at aggregate level only. No group with fewer than 10 people is reported, and exit-interview verbatims were not used in this report.
Limitations
This is an observational study. Short-notice schedule changes may partly reflect sites that are under strain in other ways, which is why we propose testing rather than rolling out. Commute distance uses home ZIP code, not actual travel mode. Exit-interview coverage is 38%, and response is skewed towards resignations.

1 Referral and prior-experience effects are shown in the chart for context. They are useful for sourcing, but they don't tell us what to change after someone is hired.
