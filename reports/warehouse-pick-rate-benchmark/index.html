Reno picks 35% more units per hour than Allentown, and most of the gap comes from slotting and pick paths, not people

A benchmark of pick productivity across our four fulfilment centres, with the gap broken down by cause and a plan for spreading Reno's practices to the other sites.

By Tom Becker Published May 20, 2026 Data through Apr 30, 2026
"We re-slot every Sunday night. It's the least glamorous thing we do, and I'd bet it's worth more than anything else."Reno FC operations manager, site visit, April 2026

The short version

  1. Pick productivity varies widely by site. Reno averaged 62 units per labour hour (UPH) from January to April. Dallas averaged 55, Columbus 51 and Allentown 46. The network averaged 53.
  2. Process explains 83% of the Reno–Allentown gap. Of the 16 UPH gap, slotting accounts for 5.8, pick-path travel for 4.3 and batch size for 3.2. Associate tenure explains only 1.8.
  3. These practices can be copied. Reno re-slots weekly by velocity, batches 10–12 orders per tote cart and uses optimised pick paths. The other three sites do each of these partly or not at all.
  4. Recommendation: move Allentown and Columbus halfway to Reno's practices before peak. That would save about 21,800 pick hours a year (≈$0.53M) and cut peak temp-labour needs. Owners: FC General Managers, Engineering & Continuous Improvement.

01Reno leads the network by a wide margin, and Allentown trails it

Every FC has the same WMS, the same tote carts and roughly the same product catalogue. Pick rate still ranges from 46 to 62 units per labour hour.

From January to April the four FCs picked 5.22M units in 98,000 pick labour hours. Reno picked 1.36M units at 62 UPH, Dallas 1.46M at 55, Columbus 1.22M at 51 and Allentown 1.17M at 46. The spread has been stable. Allentown was the slowest site in each of the last six quarters, so this is not a one-off. The ranking holds after we remove big-and-bulky units (which are handled by a separate team with its own productivity standard) and after we weight each site to the same category mix.

02Slotting, travel and batching explain 13.3 of the 16 UPH gap

We fitted a model of pick rate at the associate-shift level and used it to split the Reno–Allentown gap into its drivers.

The model is a linear mixed model with random effects for each associate, fitted to 212,400 associate-shifts. It explains 61% of the variance in shift-level UPH. The four drivers are all significant and have the expected signs. We controlled for category mix, units per order, shift (day/night) and week. Holding everything else constant:

This isn't about Allentown's people

The GM, and some of the site review decks, have put Allentown's gap down to "a newer, greener workforce". Tenure does matter, since a new picker is 14 UPH slower than a one-year veteran. But Allentown's extra share of new pickers explains only 1.8 of the 16 UPH gap. If Allentown had Reno's tenure mix tomorrow and kept its current slotting and batching, it would still be the slowest site.

03Batching is the cheapest lever, and it stops paying off at about 12 orders

Allentown's carts carry 6.4 orders on average and Reno's carry 11.2. Allentown's small batches come from a wave-release setting, not from any physical limit.

Each extra order on the cart spreads the walk across more units. The effect is strong up to about 12 orders, after which cart capacity and sort-at-pack errors take most of the gain away. At Reno, batches above 12 had a 0.9% mis-sort rate compared with 0.3% below 12. Changing Allentown's and Columbus's wave release rule to a 10–12 order target is a WMS configuration change and needs no capital.

04New pickers need about 12 weeks to reach the network average, so tenure matters most in peak

Tenure is a small part of the site gap, but it matters a lot for peak planning, when a third of the workforce will be new.

Reno ramps new pickers faster than the other sites. In a new picker's fourth week Reno measures 47 UPH, compared with 41 at Allentown. Reno pairs each new hire with a "pick buddy" for the first two weeks and starts them on single-zone batches before moving them onto full paths. That program costs about 6 hours of veteran time per new hire. The benefit is roughly 30 additional pick hours saved over the first 12 weeks1.

05Moving Allentown and Columbus halfway to Reno's practices would save ~21,800 pick hours a year

We do not assume either site can match Reno completely. Reno's building has a single-level pick module, while Allentown's has a mezzanine. Our target is for each site to close half its gap to Reno. That means Allentown from 46 to 54 UPH and Columbus from 51 to 56.5. At January–April volumes this saves 3,800 hours at Allentown and 2,300 at Columbus, or 6,100 hours in four months. January–April is about 28% of annual unit volume, so the annual saving is about 21,800 hours, or $0.53M at a $24.50 loaded rate. Nearly half of that saving would come in November and December, when each hour saved is an hour we don't have to fill with a temp.

Caveat

The model shows association at the shift level, and slotting is not randomly assigned. Before scaling, we recommend a controlled pilot: re-slot one Allentown zone and use the other zones as the comparison.

Methodology

Population
All each-pick tasks at the four FCs, Jan 5 – Apr 30, 2026 (5.22M units; 212,400 associate-shifts; 1,062 distinct pickers). Excludes big-and-bulky (team-lift) picks, replenishment and returns put-away.
Metric
UPH = units picked ÷ pick labour hours. Pick hours include direct task time plus a pro-rata share of indirect time (start-up meetings, cart staging) from Workday time clocks.
Model
Linear mixed model of shift-level UPH with associate random intercepts. Fixed effects: golden-zone share of the shift's picks, mean travel per pick (from WMS location coordinates), batch size (piecewise, knot at 12), tenure band, category mix, units per order, shift and week. Decomposition = coefficient × Reno–Allentown difference in each driver's mean.
Validation
Out-of-sample R² on May 1–10 data was 0.58. The site ranking and the size of the effects were stable when the model was fitted to each FC separately.
Limitations
Observational: better-run sites may also do other things well that we haven't measured. Travel distance is estimated from bin coordinates, not measured. Building constraints (Allentown's mezzanine) are grouped under "unexplained".

1 The difference between Reno's and the other sites' ramp curves over weeks 1–12, multiplied by average hours worked. The estimate is sensitive to early attrition, which is higher at Allentown.
