More shoppers know us; fewer pick us once they compare

Brand tracker wave 4 (June 2026, n = 2,421). Aided awareness has risen 6 points in a year, mostly from social. But we turn consideration into preference worse than any competitor, and the gap is perceived quality.

By Lena Kowalski Published Aug 20, 2026 Fieldwork Jun 8–22, 2026
“They're my go-to for things I need fast, like a lamp or bath towels. For a sofa I'd want something I trust will last ten years, and I'm not sure I'd get that from them.”Wave 4 open-end, considers Parcel & Pine but prefers Competitor B (female, 38, Chicago)

The short version

  1. Awareness is up to 44%, from 38% in wave 1. The +3-point rise since wave 3 is statistically significant. A third of recently-aware shoppers first heard of us through social ads.
  2. We lose shoppers between consideration and preference. Only 36% of those who consider us prefer us, the lowest conversion of the four brands (Competitor A: 49%).
  3. Perceived quality is the gap. We lead on fast, reliable delivery (+9 pts vs. competitors) but trail on "quality that lasts" (−6), which is the single strongest driver of preference.
  4. Competitor C is closing in. Its preference share rose from 5.0% to 6.8% over four waves and is now within half a point of ours.
  5. Recommendation: build the H2 brand platform around durability and materials proof, not only speed. Owner: Brand Marketing, creative brief by 15 Sep.

01Awareness grew 6 points in a year, matched only by Competitor C

Aided awareness among US online home-goods shoppers has climbed every wave, from 38% to 44%. Competitor A is flat near 82%, Competitor B at 55%. Competitor C is also growing, by the same 6 points, from a smaller base.

The wave 3 → 4 increase (+3 pts, 95% CI +0.2 to +5.8) is the first single-wave change large enough to rule out sampling noise. It coincides with the expanded prospecting social flight in March–May. Among respondents who say they first heard of us in the last three months (n = 212), social media ads were the most common source by a wide margin.

02Shoppers consider us, then choose someone else

Our funnel is wide at the top and narrows sharply at the bottom. 45% of those aware of us would consider us, close to the competitive average. Only 36% of those who consider us name us as their first choice.

Competitor A converts 49% of considerers into preference and Competitor C 45%. If we matched Competitor C's conversion rate, our preference share would be 9.0% instead of 7.2%. That would add roughly 1.8 points of first-choice share without gaining a single new aware shopper.

03We win on delivery and lose on quality, and quality matters more

Shoppers who know us and a competitor rate us clearly ahead on fast, reliable delivery, which reflects the Pine Fleet investment. They rate us behind on durability and sustainability.

Derived importance (the correlation of each attribute with first-choice preference) ranks "quality that lasts" first and "fast, reliable delivery" fourth. Our strongest perception sits on a mid-ranked driver, and our biggest deficit sits on the top one. That explains much of the drop from consideration to preference: shoppers put us on the list for convenience, then choose on confidence in the product.

It isn't just perception

The quality gap shows up in our own data too. The returns root-cause analysis found quality-related reasons among the leading causes of textile returns, and durability is a recurring theme in the H1 NPS detractor verbatims. Brand creative can move perception only if the product backs it up.

04Competitor C is gaining on us fastest

A year ago Competitor C had 5.0% first-choice share to our 6.1%. It now has 6.8% to our 7.2%. Both brands are growing, and C is growing faster.

Competitor C's gains are concentrated among 25–34-year-olds, where its preference (9.4%) is already above ours (8.1%). It leads us on "sustainable materials" by 15 points in that age group. Over four waves, the rise in C's preference (+1.8 pts, 95% CI +0.4 to +3.2) is significant. None of the individual wave-on-wave changes is.

05Recommendations

  1. Build the H2 brand platform around durability proof: materials, construction, warranty and long-term reviews, with speed as a supporting message rather than the headline. Owner: Brand Marketing (Carla Mendes) · Creative brief by: Sep 15, 2026
  2. Publish verifiable sustainability claims for the core furniture range (e.g., share of FSC-certified wood) before we advertise them. Owner: Merchandising & Sustainability · Claims audit by: Oct 31, 2026
  3. Keep prospecting social at the proposed higher level. It is our most effective awareness driver and is incremental (see the geo holdout). Owner: Performance Marketing · Ongoing
  4. Add a durability module and a 25–34 boost sample (n = +600) to wave 5 so we can track the quality gap and Competitor C among younger shoppers with tighter intervals. Owner: Marketing Science (Lena Kowalski) · Questionnaire lock: Aug 28, 2026
  5. Run a quarterly competitive teardown of Competitor C covering assortment, price points, sustainability claims and delivery promise. Owner: Strategy & Insights (Maya Okafor) · First readout: Oct 2026

Methodology

Population
US adults aged 25–64 who bought home goods or furniture online in the past 12 months. Recruited from a double-opt-in online panel via Qualtrics, screened for category purchase, and weighted to US Census on age, gender, region and household income.
Sample
Wave 4: n = 2,421 completes (after removing 187 speeders and straight-liners). Waves 1–3: 2,412 / 2,398 / 2,405. The questionnaire has been identical across waves. The brand list rotates randomly.
Measures
Aided awareness (brand logo and name shown), consideration ("would you consider…" for next purchase over $100), first-choice preference, and a pick-any attribute association grid. Derived importance is the point-biserial correlation of each attribute with first-choice preference, among respondents aware of at least two brands.
Significance
95% margins of error use a design effect of 1.15 for weighting. Wave-on-wave tests are two-sample tests of proportions. We call a change "significant" only at p < 0.05. With four brands and several metrics per wave, expect about one false positive per wave by chance.
Limitations
Online panel respondents skew more digitally engaged than the general population, which probably inflates awareness for online-only brands, us included. Self-reported source of awareness is subject to recall bias. Competitors are anonymised in this report per Legal guidance; the key is available on request from Marketing Science.

Competitor A is a large online generalist, Competitor B a premium omnichannel furniture brand, and Competitor C a direct-to-consumer brand founded in 2021.
