Engagement slipped 3 points, and nearly all of the drop is in our fulfilment centres

The 2026 engagement survey had our highest response rate yet (81%). Corporate engagement held steady. FC engagement fell 4 points, driven by workload and unpredictable schedules, and Dallas fell furthest.

By Hannah Liu Published Jun 25, 2026 Survey fielded May 4 – May 22, 2026

The short version

  1. 81% of employees responded (2,317 of 2,860 invited), up from 77% in 2025. FC participation reached 77%, a record, so this year's FC results are the most reliable we have had.
  2. The engagement index fell from 71% to 68% favourable. Corporate was flat at 74% (−1, within the margin of error). FCs fell from 68% to 64%, and Dallas FC fell 8 points to 59%, the lowest score of any group.
  3. Manager support, growth and workload explain 62% of the variation in engagement we can model. Growth (−4) and workload (−6) both declined. Among FC associates, schedule predictability is now the single strongest driver, and it fell 7 points company-wide.
  4. Recommendation: put the action budget behind FC scheduling and workload first (Dallas as the lead site), and make internal mobility visible for corporate teams. We will re-measure in the October pulse.

Privacy: how results are reported

All results in this report are aggregates. We never show a group with fewer than 10 respondents. Smaller teams are rolled up into their parent function, and comment themes are coded and paraphrased so no individual can be identified. Managers with 10 or more respondents receive their own team report through Culture Amp. Nobody in People Analytics or in the business can see individual answers.

01FC participation hit a record 77%, so this year's picture is the most complete we have had

The 4-point rise in response rate came almost entirely from FCs, where we added shift-start kiosk sessions and Spanish-language versions of the survey.

Corporate response was 88% (952 of 1,080 invited), in line with last year. FC response rose from 69% to 77% (1,365 of 1,780). Because the people who don't respond tend to be less engaged, better FC coverage probably pushes the reported FC score slightly lower. We estimate this effect at about 1 point of the 4-point FC decline.1 The rest is a real change. We checked this by weighting 2026 responses to 2025's tenure and shift mix, and the FC decline still came out at 3.4 points.

02Four of the ten groups we report on are flat. Dallas FC fell 8 points on its own

The company number hides a split: corporate functions all moved within ±2 points except Customer Care, while every FC went down.

Technology & Product is our most engaged group (79%, +1). Customer Care fell 4 points to 66%. Its comments point to the shift to extended weekend coverage in March. Among FCs, Reno barely moved (−1). Columbus and Allentown fell 2–3 points. Dallas fell from 67% to 59%. That fall is well outside its ±4.8-point margin of error (n = 402), and it has the lowest score in the company on four of the five drivers below.

Workload and growth are below 60 in every group, so these are company-wide issues, not FC-only ones. Schedule predictability is where the two populations differ most. Corporate teams average 67%, FCs 44%, and Dallas 34%.

03Manager support, growth and workload drive engagement. Schedule predictability matters most in the FCs

A driver score only matters for engagement if it moves engagement. Relative-weights analysis tells us how much of the explainable variation each driver accounts for.

We regressed each respondent's engagement index score on the seven driver scores and split the explained variance (R² = 0.58) with Johnson's relative weights.2 For the company as a whole, manager support accounts for 24% of what we can explain, growth & career for 21% and workload for 17%. Together those three make up 62%. They are also the drivers that moved least in our favour: manager support was down 1 point, growth down 4 and workload down 6.

When we fit the model to FC associates alone (n = 1,365), the order changes. Schedule predictability becomes the strongest driver at 22%, ahead of manager support (20%) and workload (18%). That matches what Operations Analytics sees in the attrition data. Associates whose shift changed at short notice in their first two weeks are far more likely to leave (see FC associate attrition, in progress with Tom Becker). Company-wide, predictability fell from 60% to 53% favourable. Dallas moved to a rotating weekend pattern in February and its predictability score fell 24 points (58% to 34%). That alone accounts for more than half of the company-wide fall.

Why this matters

Pay fairness (47% favourable) is the lowest-scoring item in the survey, and it is the one leaders ask about first. It is also the weakest driver of engagement (7%). Pay still matters for attraction and retention. But if the goal is to move this index, fixing schedules and workload will do more per dollar than another pay review.

04The comments point to the same causes: staffing, careers and schedules

1,142 respondents (49%) answered the open question “What one thing would most improve your experience at Parcel & Pine?”

Two coders applied a 12-code frame (inter-rater agreement κ = 0.81) and we collapsed it to the themes below. Each comment has one primary theme. The shares therefore add up to 100%.

Scheduling comments come almost entirely from FCs and Customer Care, and they are specific. People are not asking for fewer hours. They are asking to know their hours further in advance:

“I don't mind the weekend rotation. What I mind is finding out on Thursday which weekend it is.”FC associate, paraphrased from a comment in a group of 10+ respondents

Corporate career comments share a common thread: people don't know what roles exist or how to move into them. Only 31% of corporate respondents agreed with “I know how to find a new role inside Parcel & Pine”. Among people with 3+ years' tenure, 38% said they had considered leaving to get a promotion they could not see internally.

05What we recommend

The FC findings and the attrition work point in the same direction, so FCs come first. Each action has a single owner and a check-in at the October pulse survey.

  1. Publish FC schedules 14 days ahead, starting with Dallas. Move from the current 5–7 days' notice to a 14-day posted schedule with a swap board. Pilot in Dallas in Q3 and roll out to other sites if the October pulse shows predictability up by 10 points or more. Owner: Dallas FC General Manager with FC Workforce Planning · By: Aug 15, 2026
  2. Re-baseline FC staffing standards before peak. Workload scores are lowest where planned vs. actual labour hours diverged most in Q1–Q2. Feed the variance into the peak model so we don't start peak understaffed. Owner: Tom Becker (Operations Analytics) · By: Sep 1, 2026 (input to Peak 2026 capacity plan)
  3. Make internal mobility visible. Post all corporate openings internally for 5 days before external posting, and publish career frameworks for the 12 largest job families. Owner: Talent Acquisition & Talent Development · By: Oct 1, 2026
  4. Give Customer Care a workload and weekend-coverage review of its own. Its growth score of 44% and schedule score of 52% are corporate outliers. Owner: CX Ops leadership with People Partner · By: Sep 30, 2026
  5. Hold team action conversations within 30 days. Every manager with a team report (10+ respondents) runs a 45-minute results conversation and logs one commitment in Culture Amp. Owner: People Partners · By: Jul 31, 2026
How we will know it worked

October pulse (8 items, all employees): FC schedule predictability ≥ 50% favourable (from 44%), Dallas engagement ≥ 63%, corporate “I know how to find a new role” ≥ 40%. People Analytics will publish the pulse readout by Nov 6.

Methodology

Population
All active employees with at least 30 days' tenure on May 1, 2026 (2,860 invited: 1,080 corporate, 1,780 FC). Contractors and agency temps were not surveyed.
Instrument
Culture Amp engagement template (46 items) plus 4 Parcel & Pine items on scheduling and internal mobility. Available in English and Spanish, on the web and at FC kiosks. The engagement index averages 5 items (pride, recommend, motivation, intent to stay, discretionary effort), each scored as % "agree" or "strongly agree".
Analysis
Year-on-year changes are compared with 95% margins of error. Driver importance uses Johnson's relative weights on respondent-level scores, run separately for company, corporate and FC. We ran a non-response sensitivity check by weighting to the 2025 tenure and shift mix.
Privacy
Minimum reporting group of 10 respondents, applied to every cut including comments. Demographic cuts with small groups are rolled up. Raw data is held by Culture Amp. People Analytics works only with de-identified extracts.
Limitations
This is a cross-sectional survey, so the drivers show association, not proof of cause. Better FC coverage makes the 2025 and 2026 FC results slightly less comparable. Comments are self-selected: people with strong views are more likely to write one.

1 Estimated by comparing 2025 early vs. late responders (late responders scored 3.8 points lower) and applying that gap to this year's additional FC responses.

2 Johnson, J. W. (2000), “A heuristic method for estimating the relative weight of predictor variables in multiple regression.” Relative weights handle the strong correlation between drivers better than raw regression coefficients do.
