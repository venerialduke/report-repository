App search fails customers who say “couch” — synonyms and typos cause 61% of null results

The app still runs on the old search index that web moved off last year. Porting web's synonym and typo handling to the app is worth about $5M a year.

By Priya Natarajan & Aisha Rahman Published Aug 27, 2026 Data Jun 1 – Jul 31, 2026 · 12 usability sessions Jul 13–17
“I typed couch and it showed me covers. So I figured you don't sell couches and went to look somewhere else.”Participant P7, Android, sofa task — usability session, July 15

The short version

  1. App search does noticeably worse than web on every quality metric. 8.7% of app queries return nothing (web: 5.2%). Top-3 click-through is 31% (web: 38%), and 22% of searches are reformulated (web: 15%).
  2. Most nulls are vocabulary problems, not catalogue gaps. Synonym mismatches (“couch” vs “sofa”, “comforter” vs “duvet”) cause 34% of null results and misspellings cause 27%. Only 18% are for things we don't sell.
  3. Usability sessions show why it matters. 7 of 12 participants typed “couch” first, and 4 of those 7 concluded we don't sell sofas.
  4. Recommendation: port web's 412-pair synonym list and typo tolerance to the app index by October 15, ahead of peak. That recovers about $5.1M a year (range $2.0–8.0M) while the full migration to the v2 search service is pulled forward to Q1 2027.

01App search is worse than web on all three quality measures, and has been all summer

Search matters more in the app than anywhere else. 44% of app sessions include a search, compared with 29% on web.

We analysed 9.8 million app queries from 4.1 million search sessions across June and July. We tracked the three metrics the Search & Discovery squad already uses for web. Null-result rate is the share of queries that return zero products. Top-3 click-through is the share of queries where the customer clicks one of the first three results. Reformulation rate is the share of queries followed within 60 seconds by a different query with no click in between. The app was worse than web on all three.

This is not a seasonal blip. The app's weekly null rate stayed between 8.3% and 9.1% for the whole period and never came near the 5% target the squad set for web in 2025. The reason is technical. When web moved to the v2 search service in October 2025, it gained a curated synonym dictionary and fuzzy matching. The app still queries the v1 index, which matches exact tokens after stemming only.1

02Six in ten null results are customers using a different word, or misspelling ours

We have what these customers want. We just don't recognise the words they use for it.

We took a stratified random sample of 3,000 null-result app queries and hand-coded each one. Two coders worked on the sample and agreed 91% of the time (κ = 0.86). Synonym mismatches were the largest group, followed by misspellings. Together they account for 61% of nulls. Queries for products we genuinely don't carry, such as appliances and outdoor grills, are only 18%.

The pattern is consistent. Our catalogue uses trade and UK-leaning terms (sofa, duvet, cushion, bedside table), while many US customers use everyday words (couch, comforter, throw pillow, nightstand). Web already maps all of these pairs. The app maps none of them.

03In the lab, a failed first search reads as “they don't sell it”

The usability sessions show what the logs can't: a null result does not look like a search problem to customers. It looks like a missing product.

Aisha Rahman ran 12 moderated remote sessions (6 iOS, 6 Android; 8 existing customers and 4 non-customers who shop online for furniture). Each participant did three search tasks on a production build. We did not coach them on wording.

Why this matters more than the null rate suggests

Nine of the 12 participants said they would expect a competitor's app to “just know” that couch means sofa. When search failed, none of them tapped the “Browse categories” link on the empty-results screen, because it sits below the fold. The empty state is effectively a dead end.

04Fixing vocabulary nulls is worth about $5M a year

Sessions that hit a null result convert at a third of the rate of sessions where the customer reformulated and then clicked.

In a typical month, 2.05M app search sessions include 238k (11.6%) that hit at least one null. Synonyms and typos cause 61% of those, or 145k sessions. We assume the fix resolves 75% of them (109k sessions). If those sessions go on to convert like “reformulated, then clicked” sessions (4.2% instead of 1.3%), that adds about 3,160 orders a month. At the app AOV of $134, that is $423k a month, or about $5.1M a year. Our low case (50% resolved, converting at 3.0%) gives $2.0M. Our high case (90% resolved, converting at 5.1%) gives $8.0M. None of these figures include the likely gain in top-3 click-through, and none account for peak, when app search volume roughly doubles.

Caveat

This is an opportunity estimate, not a measured effect. We will confirm it by releasing the synonym port behind a 50/50 flag for two weeks in October. The pre-registration is already drafted (see the experimentation playbook).

05Recommendations

  1. Port web's synonym dictionary (412 pairs) to the app's v1 index. Search Platform estimates 1.5 sprints, and it removes the largest class of nulls. Owner: Search & Discovery squad · By: Oct 15, 2026 (behind a 50/50 flag)
  2. Turn on edit-distance-1 typo tolerance for queries of 5 characters or more. This covers the most common misspellings with low false-match risk. Owner: Search Platform · By: Oct 15, 2026
  3. Redesign the empty-results screen. Show “Did you mean…” and the top three categories above the fold. Owner: App squad with Aisha Rahman · By: Nov 6, 2026 (before Black Friday code freeze)
  4. Move the app to the v2 search service in Q1 2027 instead of Q3. This retires the dual-index maintenance cost for good. Owner: Head of Digital Product · Decision by: Sep 30, 2026 planning
  5. Add null-rate, top-3 CTR and reformulation to the weekly app scorecard. Owner: Jonah Feld · By: Sep 15, 2026

Methodology

Log analysis
All US app and web search queries from Jun 1 to Jul 31, 2026, excluding bot-flagged traffic (0.9%) and internal accounts. A “session” is a 30-minute inactivity window. Metrics follow the Search & Discovery squad's definitions (dw.fct_search_queries, v3). Root-cause coding used a stratified random sample of 3,000 null queries, weighted by volume decile, coded independently by two analysts (κ = 0.86), with disagreements resolved by discussion.
Usability study
12 moderated remote sessions, 45 minutes each, recruited via UserInterviews. 8 existing customers and 4 non-customers; 6 iOS and 6 Android. Same three tasks for everyone, with the task order rotated. The sessions show how and why search fails. They are too small a sample to estimate how often it happens.
Opportunity sizing
Monthly averages from Jun–Jul. Counterfactual conversion is taken from observed outcome groups, which are affected by self-selection, so we report a range and will validate with an experiment.
Limitations
Two summer months including Pine Days. Assumes app AOV ($134) is unchanged. Does not cover voice search (under 1% of queries) or the marketplace channel.

1 The v1 index applies Porter stemming, so “sofas” matches “sofa”, but it has no synonym expansion and no fuzzy matching. See Search Platform's ADR-031 (Oct 2025) for the web migration.
