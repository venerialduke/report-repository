Single-page checkout lifts conversion 5.6% — but the win is on mobile web, not the app

Four weeks, 620k sessions: ship the new checkout to web now, and give the app team one more iteration before it goes to 100%.

By Priya Natarajan Published Jun 18, 2026 Test ran May 4 – May 31, 2026

The short version

  1. The single-page checkout converts better. Session conversion rose from 3.40% to 3.59%, a +5.6% relative lift (95% CI +2.9% to +8.3%). The result held in every week of the test and cleared our pre-registered 3.5% minimum detectable effect.
  2. Almost all of the lift comes from mobile web (+14.2%). Desktop web is positive but not conclusive (+5.0%). The iOS and Android apps are flat, and their intervals sit either side of zero.
  3. Guardrails held, with one thing to watch. Average order value dipped 1.3% (not significant), mostly because fewer customers added protection plans. Support contacts fell by 7%. Payment errors went up on Android only.
  4. Recommendation: ship to 100% of web traffic from June 23, and run a v2 app test with protection-plan placement and Android address fixes. FP&A should plan on about $20M of annualised web revenue, not the $53M point estimate.

01The single-page checkout wins, and the result is robust

The Checkout squad designed the test to answer one question: does collapsing shipping, payment and review into one scrolling page get more people from cart to placed order? It does.

We randomised at the customer level (logged-in ID, falling back to device ID) and split 620,000 checkout-eligible sessions evenly: 310,212 saw the existing three-step flow and 309,788 saw the single page. The treatment converted 3.59% of sessions against 3.40% for control. That is +0.19 percentage points (95% CI +0.10 to +0.28 pp), or +5.6% relative. The sample-ratio check passed (p = 0.43) and pre-period conversion was identical between arms. We pre-registered the test on April 28 with a 3.5% relative MDE and a fixed four-week horizon, and we did not peek at the primary metric before May 31.1

The gap narrowed from +0.28 pp in week 1 to +0.14 pp in week 4. Some of that is novelty wearing off: returning customers who already knew the old flow explored the new one more at first. We cannot yet tell whether the effect will settle at the week-4 level or keep fading. That uncertainty is why the revenue number in section 04 carries a heavy haircut.

02Mobile web drives the lift; the app is neutral

The old three-step flow hurt most on small screens in a browser. Each step was a full page reload, and each reload was another chance to lose the customer.

We pre-registered platform as the one segment cut we would read. Mobile web, which is 38% of checkout sessions and has the lowest baseline conversion (2.6%), improved by +14.2%. Desktop web improved by +5.0%, but its interval includes zero. Both native apps were effectively flat. That fits the design: the apps already used an in-app sheet with no page reloads between steps, so there was much less friction to remove.

03Customers get further through checkout, and guardrails mostly hold

The new page cuts drop-off at every stage. The largest gain is at address entry, which the new flow autocompletes inline.

Average order value fell 1.3%, which is inside our −3% non-inferiority margin. The cause is clear. The old step 2 showed the protection-plan offer on a page of its own, and the single page puts it in a collapsed row. Protection-plan attach fell from 6.8% to 5.9% of eligible orders, which accounts for about $1.40 of the $1.90 AOV gap. Merchandising has already asked for a v2 with the offer shown inline, and we think most of the gap can be recovered.

Payment errors rose slightly overall. Web and iOS were flat; all of the increase came from Android. There, the new address component sometimes sends a ZIP+4 format that our payment processor's AVS check rejects. The Android error rate went from 2.0% to 2.9%. That probably explains why Android shows the only negative point estimate. The Payments team confirmed the bug on June 9 and a fix is in the 7.14 release.

Support contacts fell 7%. CX Ops tagging shows fewer "where do I enter my promo code" and "my address won't save" contacts. On the old flow, both came from state getting lost between steps.

What this test does not tell us

The test ran in May, outside peak and before Pine Days. Holiday customers are more often first-time, gift-buying and on mobile web, so the effect could be larger at peak. It could also be smaller if the page slows down under load. We will keep a 5% holdout on web through Cyber Week so we can check.

04Plan on $20M a year from web, not $53M

The point estimate is large. We would rather FP&A plan on a number we expect to beat.

Web accounts for about 52% of orders, or roughly $645M of the 2026 plan. In the test, web revenue per session rose 8.2% (conversion +9.6%, AOV −1.3%). Applied naively to all web revenue, that is about $53M a year. We recommend a planning figure of $20M, for three reasons. The week-4 effect is already about half the week-1 effect. Our tests have historically shrunk by 30–50% after full rollout (see the experimentation playbook). And the lower end of the web CI supports only about $8M. We will re-measure against the web holdout at the end of Q3.

Methodology

Design
Two-arm randomised controlled test (CHK-212), 50/50 split, randomised at the customer level, with device ID as the fallback for guests. Pre-registered on Apr 28, 2026: primary metric, guardrails with margins, a single segment cut (platform), and a 3.5% relative MDE at 80% power (with CUPED), which required about 300k sessions per arm.
Population
Sessions that reached cart with at least one in-stock item, on web and on app versions 7.11 and later, in the US. Marketplace orders and employee accounts were excluded. 620,000 sessions from 511,400 customers.
Period
May 4 – May 31, 2026 (4 full weeks).
Analysis
Difference in proportions with delta-method standard errors, clustered by customer. Revenue metrics winsorised at the 99.9th percentile. CUPED adjustment using each customer's 28-day pre-period conversion, which reduced variance by 18%. Segment CIs are not adjusted for multiplicity, but the mobile web result survives a Bonferroni correction across the four platforms.
Limitations
Off-peak period. Novelty effects are visible and may continue. AOV and payment errors had less power than conversion. Revenue projection assumes 2026 plan traffic and does not include lifetime value effects.

1 Guardrails were monitored daily for safety (automatic stop if payment errors rose more than 1 pp). Checking a guardrail does not inflate the false-positive rate on the primary metric.
