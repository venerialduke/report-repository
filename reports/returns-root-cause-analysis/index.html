Half of what returns cost us comes from two causes we can fix: damage in transit and product photos that mislead

A mixed-methods look at H1 2026 returns: 889k returned units, $29.4M in cost, and about $5.6M a year that three changes could save.

By Tom Becker & Aisha Rahman Published Aug 12, 2026 Data through Jun 30, 2026
"On the site it looked like a warm oatmeal. What turned up was grey. I don't mind paying for nice things, but I want to know what I'm buying."Customer interview #17, returned a bouclé accent chair, June 2026

The short version

  1. Returns cost $29.4M in H1. 11.0% of units shipped came back (889k). Furniture is 8% of returned units but 42% of the cost, because each furniture return costs $182 to process.
  2. Damage in transit is the most expensive reason. It is 21% of returned units and 31% of return cost. Most of that cost comes from flat-pack furniture shipped by carrier in a carton, and from fragile items packed on the auto-bagger lines at Allentown and Columbus.
  3. "Didn't match description" traces back to product photos. SKUs without a colour-calibrated swatch or a scale shot are returned for this reason at 2–3× the rate of SKUs with a full image set. The interviews explain why: colour and size were the two things customers said the photos got wrong.
  4. Recommendation: repack fragile SKUs, protect or reroute the furniture most prone to damage, and reshoot 1,200 SKUs. The expected net saving is about $5.6M a year. Owners: FC Packaging Engineering, Big & Bulky Logistics, Creative Studio.

01Reasons are spread evenly by unit, but damage costs far more than anything else

No single reason code accounts for more than 22% of returned units. When we weight by cost, damage in transit accounts for almost a third.

Counted by units, "didn't match description" (21.7%), "changed my mind" (21.3%) and "damaged in transit" (20.8%) are close to level. Cost is a different picture. The average damaged return costs $50, compared with $33 across all returns, because damage is concentrated in heavy and fragile goods. Those items need a reverse pickup, can't be resold and are often replaced at our expense. Damage accounts for $9.27M of the $29.4M. "Changed my mind" is the reason we can do least about, and it is also the cheapest per unit, because most of it is textiles that go straight back to stock.

02Each category has a different main reason, so a single returns programme won't fit them all

Damage dominates furniture and lighting. Description and size problems dominate rugs and textiles.

The reason picker undercounts the causes we can fix

We coded a random sample of 3,850 free-text return comments. In 18% of the "changed my mind" returns, the comment actually described a description or damage problem ("smaller than I thought", "chipped on one corner"). The customer picked the easiest option. The true share of damage and description returns is therefore probably 2–4 pp higher than shown. Our savings estimates use the reason codes as recorded, so they are conservative.

03Most damage comes from flat-pack furniture sent by carrier and fragile items on two auto-bagger lines

About 61% of damage cost is furniture ($5.70M). Most of the rest is lighting and ceramics, and both problems come down to a few specific ways we ship.

Big & bulky. About 38% of furniture units are flat-pack items that go by national carrier in a carton, not by white-glove. Those units come back damaged at 58 per 1,000 shipped. White-glove deliveries, which are blanket-wrapped and carried in by two people, come back damaged at 16.5 per 1,000. That comes to about 21.4k damaged returns and $3.9M of damage cost in H1, or 68% of all furniture damage cost. The 150 worst SKUs account for 58% of that. Most are tall case goods (bookcases, wardrobes) weighing over 70 lb, which carrier sort equipment handles badly.

Fragile goods. Lighting and glass or ceramic decor (1.62M units in H1) come back damaged at 55 per 1,000 overall. The rate depends heavily on how they are packed. Allentown Line 4 and Columbus Line 2 run automated baggers that put bubble-lined poly mailers on anything under the size limit, and that includes lamps and ceramics. Those units are damaged at 92 per 1,000, which is eight times the rate for double-wall cartons with molded-pulp inserts.

04"Didn't match description" follows the gaps in our product photos

In the interviews, customers didn't say the product was bad. They said it wasn't what the photos had shown them.

Aisha interviewed 24 customers who had returned an item as "didn't match description" in May or June. Fourteen said the colour was different from what they expected, 11 said the item was smaller or larger than they had pictured, and 8 mentioned material or texture. Several named more than one. Nobody said the listing text was wrong. Most hadn't read it. Customers relied on the images, and the images were missing two things: a colour-accurate swatch and a shot that showed the item next to something for scale.

We then checked this against the catalogue. Using image metadata in dw.dim_product, we grouped every SKU by photo completeness. SKUs with a full set (six or more images, a scale or lifestyle shot and a colour-calibrated swatch) have a description-return rate of 1.4% of units shipped. SKUs missing only the scale shot come in at 2.6%, those missing the calibrated swatch at 3.3%, and those with three or fewer images at 3.9%. The pattern holds within each category and price band.

05Three changes would save about $5.6M a year, net of what they cost

We sized each action on H1 volumes and doubled the result to get a full year. Peak probably makes that an underestimate.

Damage is the second most common detractor theme in the H1 NPS verbatims. The photo reshoot cost is a one-off, so from year two the net saving on that line rises to the full $2.7M.

Where to start

Start with the fragile repack. It needs no new vendors, since double-wall cartons and pulp inserts are already stocked at all four FCs. It is a routing rule change in the pack-station logic, and a two-week A/B by SKU on Allentown Line 4 would confirm the effect before peak.

06Recommendations

  1. Route fragile SKUs away from the auto-baggers. Flag lighting and glass or ceramic SKUs as "fragile" in the item master and send them to double-wall and pulp packing. Run an A/B on Allentown Line 4 first. Owner: FC Packaging Engineering · By: A/B live Aug 31; network rollout Oct 15, 2026
  2. Protect or reroute the 150 flat-pack furniture SKUs with the most damage. Add corner and edge protection to the rest of the carton-shipped furniture range. Owner: Big & Bulky Logistics with Merchandising · By: Oct 31, 2026
  3. Reshoot 1,200 SKUs, ranked by expected avoided returns. Make a scale shot and calibrated swatch mandatory before any new SKU goes live. Owner: Creative Studio with Site Merchandising · By: first 400 SKUs by Oct 1; all by Dec 15, 2026
  4. Redesign the return-reason picker. Ask one follow-up question when a customer picks "changed my mind", so the causes we can fix are no longer hidden. Owner: Aisha Rahman (UX Research) with Post-Purchase Product · By: Sep 30, 2026
  5. Add a monthly returns cost dashboard by reason, pack spec and photo completeness. Owner: Jonah Feld (Analytics Engineering) · By: Sep 15, 2026

Methodology

Population
All units shipped Jan 1 – Jun 30, 2026 from our four FCs (8.1M) and returns initiated against them by Jul 31 (889.3k), so each shipment had a return window of at least 30 days. Marketplace-fulfilled orders are excluded.
Cost of returns
Reverse freight, returns processing labour (from Workday time allocated to returns), inventory write-down for non-resaleable units, and replacement shipments. Customer refunds are not counted as a cost.
Quantitative
Descriptive rates by reason, category, pack spec and photo completeness. For photo effects, a logistic regression of description returns on image features with category, price band and FC controls.
Qualitative
24 semi-structured remote interviews (45 min) by Aisha Rahman, May 27 – Jun 20, 2026, recruited from recent description returners across all categories. Also 3,850 randomly sampled free-text return comments, double-coded (κ = 0.81).
Savings
H1 volumes × expected change in rate × cost per return, doubled for a full year. Implementation costs from Packaging Engineering and Creative Studio quotes.
Limitations
Reason codes are self-reported and noisy (see section 2). Photo completeness is correlated with SKU age and vendor, which we control for only in part. Interview participants are self-selected and are not a representative sample.
