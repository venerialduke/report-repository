Only Pine Fleet meets the 95% on-time promise, and padding our delivery dates has hidden how far carriers have slipped

On-time delivery across the network was 92.1% from January to August. Adding buffer days to the promise made the headline number look better, cost us conversion and left the late tail where it was.

By Tom Becker Published Sep 4, 2026 Data through Aug 31, 2026

The short version

  1. Only Pine Fleet meets the SLA. Our in-house fleet delivered 96.4% of parcels by the promised date. National carriers managed 90.8% and white-glove partners 84.1%, so the network as a whole reached 92.1% against a 95% target.
  2. The gap comes from the carriers, not the metros. In the same six metros and the same ZIP codes, Pine Fleet beats the national carriers by 3.9 to 5.8 pp.
  3. Padding the promise hid a ~4 pp decline in carrier performance. Since March we have added a buffer day on 38% of national-carrier volume. Measured against their unpadded transit standard, the carriers fell from 92.1% to 86.0%. The padded lanes also lost 1.2% of checkout conversion (≈$3.6M a year).
  4. Late parcels cost us NPS. Late deliveries are 7.9% of parcels but 23% of transactional-NPS detractors. When a parcel is two or more days late, NPS drops from +52 to −8.
  5. Recommendation: remove padding lane by lane, hold carriers to scan-based SLAs with penalties, and grow Pine Fleet ZIP coverage in Chicago and Philadelphia first. Owners: Delivery Experience, Transportation, CX Ops.

01Pine Fleet has stayed above 95% all year. National carriers and white-glove have both drifted down since March

The network missed the 95% SLA in every month of 2026. Pine Fleet cleared it every month, including the July Pine Days peak. The two partner channels are moving the wrong way.

From January to August we delivered 7.08M parcels: 2.19M by Pine Fleet (31%), 4.46M by national carriers (63%) and 0.43M big-and-bulky shipments by white-glove partners (6%). Pine Fleet's monthly on-time rate stayed between 95.8% and 96.9%. The national carriers fell from 92.4% in January to 88.9% in July and recovered only to 90.1% in August. White-glove fell further, from 86.2% to a low of 82.7% in June. Because national carriers carry nearly two-thirds of our volume, their slide drives the network number. A 1 pp change in carrier on-time moves the network by about 0.6 pp.

02Carriers are 4–6 pp behind Pine Fleet even on the same streets

One explanation for Pine Fleet's lead would be that it only serves dense, easy metros. The data doesn't support that.

National carriers still deliver about 18% of parcels inside the six Pine Fleet metros, either as overflow or for ZIPs at the edge of fleet coverage. When we compare the two channels on the same metro, ZIP-code ring and parcel size class, Pine Fleet is ahead by 3.9 to 5.8 pp. Chicago and Philadelphia have the largest gaps and are also the two metros where Pine Fleet is itself closest to the SLA line. The cause is capacity. Fleet routes there are at 94–97% of planned stops on Mondays, so some Monday overflow goes to carriers, and those overflow parcels are late 11% of the time.

03Padding the promise lifted the headline rate by ~4 pp and hid the carriers' decline

From March, the Delivery Promise team added one buffer day to national-carrier lanes that had missed 92% in the previous four weeks. That rule now applies to 38% of national-carrier volume.

The padding did what it was designed to do on paper. In the padded lanes, on-time against the customer promise rose by 2.1 pp in the first month. It did not change carrier performance. When we measure the same parcels against the carrier's unpadded scan-based transit standard (the date we would have promised without the buffer), national-carrier on-time fell from 92.1% in January to 86.0% in August. Against the padded promise the fall was only to 90.1%. The ~4 pp difference is decline we stopped seeing.

Padding also has a cost that the delivery dashboards don't show. A longer promise at checkout loses orders. We compared padded lanes with similar unpadded lanes before and after March (difference-in-differences, weekly lane panels). Checkout conversion in padded lanes fell by 1.2% relative (95% CI −0.4% to −2.0%). Padded lanes carry about 24% of our orders, so the loss is roughly $3.6M in annualised revenue. That is about what Transportation would pay out in a year of late-delivery credits on the same parcels.

Why padding can't fix this

A buffer day helps only when a parcel is exactly one day late. In 2026, 61% of our late parcels were two or more days late. Most of those are lost-in-network, sort-centre missorts and failed first attempts, and one extra day would not have saved them. The padding moved the promise date, but the late parcels are still late.

04Late parcels are 8% of deliveries and 23% of detractors

We linked 61,200 transactional NPS responses to the delivery outcome of the order they rated.

Customers whose parcel arrived on time gave an NPS of +52. One day late brought it down to +31, and two or more days late to −8. Late parcels make up 7.9% of deliveries (3.1% one day late, 4.8% two or more), but because the detractor rate for 2+-days-late orders is 41%, they account for about 23% of all delivery-related detractors. This matches the "late and no update" theme in the H1 NPS verbatim analysis. There, customers were more upset about how little they were told than about the delay.

"The date moved twice and nobody told me. I took a day off for a sofa that turned up on Thursday instead of Monday."Detractor verbatim, white-glove delivery, Phoenix, July 2026

05Where Pine Fleet operates, it costs less per parcel and delivers more on time

In most networks, reliability costs extra. Here the cheaper channel is also the more reliable one.

In its metros Pine Fleet costs $6.85 per parcel, fully loaded (drivers, vans, fuel, sort labour, depot lease). National carriers cost $7.60 on average, before peak and residential surcharges. White-glove costs much more because each stop is a scheduled two-person delivery. At $58.40 a stop and 84.1% on time, white-glove is our most expensive channel and our least reliable one.

Carrier A and Carrier B are the two national contracts. Both renew in Q1 2027, and neither contract currently puts penalties on on-time performance1. Expanding Pine Fleet coverage by 15% of ZIPs in Chicago and Philadelphia would move about 240k parcels a year from carriers to the fleet. Using the per-parcel costs above, that saves about $180k a year in delivery cost. On its own it adds only ~0.1 pp to network on-time, so the carriers remain the bigger lever.

What this doesn't tell us

We don't observe the carriers' internal network, so we can't yet separate their own sort-centre delays from late handoffs by our FCs. About 9% of late carrier parcels had a first carrier scan more than 12 hours after our dispatch cut-off, which points to our dock. Transportation is pulling trailer departure logs so we can split that out.

06Recommendations

  1. Remove promise padding lane by lane, with a guardrail. Take the buffer off lanes where the carrier is meeting 93% or more against the unpadded standard, and send late-risk notifications instead. Expected result: most of the 1.2% conversion loss in those lanes comes back (≈$2M+ a year) for under 1 pp of reported on-time. Owner: Delivery Promise (Product) with Transportation · By: Oct 15, 2026
  2. Report the unpadded on-time rate. Put "on-time vs. unpadded standard" next to the customer-promise rate on the weekly ops dashboard so a carrier decline can't be absorbed by padding again. Owner: Jonah Feld (Analytics Engineering) · By: Sep 30, 2026
  3. Negotiate scan-based SLAs with penalties in the 2027 carrier renewals. Target ≥94% against the transit standard, with credits at 1% of spend per pp below target. Owner: Transportation Procurement · By: Jan 31, 2027
  4. Add Monday capacity to Pine Fleet in Chicago and Philadelphia, then extend ZIP coverage. Four more Monday routes per metro would absorb the overflow that currently goes to carriers late. Owner: Pine Fleet Ops · By: Nov 1, 2026 (before peak)
  5. Start a white-glove partner review. Make proactive rescheduling messages mandatory and add a partner scorecard to the quarterly business review. Owner: CX Ops with Big & Bulky Logistics · By: Oct 31, 2026

Methodology

Population
All customer parcels delivered Jan 1 – Aug 31, 2026 (7.08M). Excludes marketplace orders fulfilled by the marketplace (≈10% of orders), returns and store transfers.
On-time definition
First "delivered" carrier or fleet scan on or before the promised date shown at checkout (local time). For white-glove, "delivered" means the completed-installation event.
Unpadded standard
promise_days_raw from the promise engine, i.e. the carrier transit standard plus FC handling time, before the lane buffer rule was applied.
Metro comparison
Carrier and fleet parcels matched on metro, ZIP ring (distance from depot) and parcel size class. Differences shown are simple rates within matched cells, weighted to fleet volume.
Conversion impact
Difference-in-differences on weekly lane-level checkout conversion, 118 padded vs. 164 comparable unpadded lanes, Jan 5 – Aug 30. Pre-trends were parallel (p = 0.62). Standard errors clustered by lane.
NPS link
Post-delivery survey responses (Qualtrics) joined on order_id. The response rate is 4.1%, and late-delivery customers are slightly more likely to respond, so we reweighted by outcome.
Limitations
Carrier scans can be delayed or falsified ("delivered" before arrival). We estimate this at under 0.5% from customer "not received" contacts. Cost per parcel for Pine Fleet uses full allocation, so marginal costs are lower.

1 Current contracts include only a guaranteed-service refund on express tiers, which make up under 2% of our volume.
