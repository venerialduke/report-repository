Late and damaged deliveries cost us 13 NPS points; product and design earn them back

Relationship NPS fell to +38 in H1 2026. Coding 14,120 verbatims shows that customers love what we sell and are let down by how it gets to them.

By Aisha Rahman Published Jul 30, 2026 Fieldwork Jan 5 – Jun 28, 2026
“The sideboard is gorgeous, honestly the nicest piece in our house. It just took three delivery windows and arrived with a cracked corner. I’d buy from you again, but I wouldn’t tell my sister to.”Detractor (score 5), Pine+ member, Columbus OH, May 2026

The short version

  1. NPS is +38, down 3 points from H2 2025 (+41). The fall came from both Pine+ members (+52) and non-members (+29), and was driven by more detractors rather than fewer promoters.
  2. Delivery drives detractors. Delivery reliability comes up in 41% of detractor comments and damaged items in 29%. Customers whose order arrived late and damaged score −46.
  3. Product drives promoters. Product quality (44%) and design & style (37%) are the top promoter themes. Delivery is rarely why people recommend us.
  4. If every order arrived on time and undamaged, NPS would be about +51. Most of the 13-point gap is on-time delivery (5.5 pts). Damage accounts for 4.3 pts and orders that were both late and damaged for 2.9 pts. Owners: Last-mile ops, Packaging, CX Ops.

01NPS slipped 3 points in H1, and members and non-members fell together

The fall is not about who we surveyed or when. It shows up in every quarter of the half and in both member groups, which suggests something in the experience changed rather than the sample.

Relationship NPS is +38 for H1 2026: 54% promoters, 30% passives and 16% detractors (n = 21,860; 95% CI ±1.2 points). That is down from +41 in H2 2025. The change is statistically clear (p < 0.001) and big enough to matter. It is also the first half-on-half fall since we began the relationship programme in 2023. Q2 was weaker than Q1 (+37 against +39), and the fall coincides with the March increase in the non-member free-shipping threshold and a rise in late deliveries in the Dallas and Phoenix Pine Fleet metros.

02Detractors talk about delivery; promoters talk about the product

We coded all 14,120 verbatims into an 11-theme codebook (method below). A comment can have more than one theme, so the percentages don’t add to 100. Seven themes cover 94% of comments. The split between promoters and detractors is very clear:

Two trends stand out. Mentions of delivery reliability rose 5 pp, with “rescheduled” and “missed window” the fastest-growing phrases. That fits the findings of the free-shipping threshold elasticity report.

03Linking survey responses to order data puts a number on the cost of delivery failures

Verbatims tell us what customers say. Linking each response to that customer’s most recent order tells us what actually happened, and how much it moved their score.

We matched 20,410 respondents (93%) to their latest delivered order in dw.fct_shipments and grouped them by what happened to it. Of these, 78% had an order that was on time and undamaged, and they gave us an NPS of +51. The 13% whose order was late scored +9. The 6% who received a damaged item scored −21. The 3% whose order was both late and damaged scored −46, with six in ten of them detractors.

To estimate how many NPS points each failure costs, we asked what overall NPS would be if every customer in that group had scored like the on-time, undamaged group. Summed together, delivery failures account for 12.7 points, the whole gap between our actual +38 and a flawless-delivery +51.

So what

Our NPS problem is an operations problem that shows up in a CX survey. The product is doing its job: even detractors praise it (“the rug is lovely, when it finally came”). Every point we win back on delivery reliability counts directly toward NPS.

04Recommendations

  1. Make on-time-in-window the headline last-mile KPI for H2, reported by metro, with Dallas and Phoenix Pine Fleet as named focus areas. Share this NPS link with the last-mile delivery SLA review team so SLA targets are tied to customer impact. Owner: Last-mile ops (Tom Becker for analytics) · By: Aug 31, 2026
  2. Target packaging fixes at the SKUs with the most damage. 22 SKUs, mostly case goods and glass, account for 38% of damage-coded verbatims that name a product. Route this list to the Packaging engineering backlog. Owner: Packaging & FC ops · Pilot by: Sep 30, 2026
  3. Contact customers proactively when a delivery slips. Detractors who say they were “never told” about a delay score 14 points lower than other late-delivery detractors. Send an automatic SMS and email with a new window as soon as a route is re-planned. Owner: CX Ops · Launch by: Oct 15, 2026 (before peak)
  4. Use product love in marketing. Promoter verbatims about quality and design are strong, specific proof points. With consent, pass the top 200 to Brand for creative and product-page reviews. Owner: Brand marketing with UX Research · By: Sep 15, 2026

Methodology

Survey
Qualtrics relationship NPS, sent quarterly to a random sample of active customers (purchased in last 12 months), stratified by member status and region. Response rate 9.4%. Results are weighted to the active-customer base.
Coding
A hybrid approach. (1) BERTopic clustering on sentence embeddings produced 22 candidate topics. (2) Two researchers merged these into an 11-theme codebook with sentiment. (3) Two coders labelled 1,500 verbatims by hand (inter-rater κ = 0.84). (4) An LLM classifier prompted with the codebook labelled the full set. Its agreement with the human labels was κ = 0.81 overall and ≥ 0.76 on every theme. A 5% sample of the classifier’s labels is audited each wave.
Linkage
Respondents were matched to their most recent delivered order before the survey date (93% match rate). Delivery status comes from carrier and Pine Fleet scan events.
Limitations
Only 65% of respondents leave a comment, and detractors comment more often (75%) than promoters (68%), so theme shares are shares of comments, not of customers. NPS points lost are associational. The most-recent-order link ignores earlier orders that may have shaped the customer’s view.
